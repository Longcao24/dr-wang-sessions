REASON FOR VISIT
Low back pain

SUBJECTIVE
66-year-old female with a history of work-related injury in 2007 presents for ongoing management of chronic low back pain. Pain remains significant, located in the low back with radiation into the thighs and legs, right greater than left. She notes weight gain. Pain has become more tolerable than previously, but she continues to use a cane for ambulation. No bladder or bowel incontinence.

She is not able to tolerate clonidine due to blood pressure instability, with hypotension when taking clonidine, and agreed to discontinue it.

OBJECTIVE
Physical Exam:
General Appearance: Morbid obesity, no acute distress, appropriately dressed
Abdominal: Abdomen clean
Musculoskeletal: Decreased active ROM of low back, pelvic crest height discrepancy with left lower than right by about 1.25 inches, significant tenderness over bilateral sacroiliac joints right greater than left, tenderness over mid back and low back paraspinal muscles
Neurological: Straight leg raise negative, no atrophy of bilateral lower extremities

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN
66-year-old female with chronic pain after a work-related injury in 2007 presents with persistent low back pain with right-predominant radiation and exam notable for decreased lumbar ROM, pelvic crest height discrepancy, and bilateral SI joint tenderness. No bowel or bladder incontinence reported.

1. CHRONIC LOW BACK PAIN WITH SCIATICA
Chronic low back pain with radiation to thighs/legs, right greater than left, with decreased lumbar active ROM and paraspinal tenderness; straight leg raise negative
 -Provide education and counseling regarding chronic pain management
 -Continue buprenorphine 300 mcg daily
 -Renew oxycodone
 -Perform manual manipulation and stretching of mid back and low back
 -Continue home exercise program

2. SACROILIAC JOINT PAIN
Bilateral SI joint tenderness, right greater than left, with pelvic crest height discrepancy
 -Perform bilateral sacroiliac joint manual manipulation
 -Instruct and reinforce trunk muscle strengthening, self-stretching, and SI joint rehabilitation exercises
 -Continue home exercise program

3. ANXIETY
Chronic pain-related anxiety discussed as a contributor to pain tolerance
 -Increase escitalopram from 10 mg per day to 20 mg per day
 -Renew naltrexone

4. ADVERSE EFFECT OF CLONIDINE
Clonidine associated with hypotension and blood pressure instability
 -Discontinue clonidine

5. VITAMIN D DEFICIENCY
Vitamin D deficiency discussed in the context of multiple joint pain and fibromyalgia pattern pain
 -Continue vitamin D supplements

Follow-up/Disposition:
- Follow up in 2 to 3 months to re-evaluate treatment response and adjust medications accordingly
