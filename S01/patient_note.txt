REASON FOR VISIT
Chronic pain follow up

SUBJECTIVE
54-55-year-old female with chronic pain on long-term opioid therapy for at least 20 years presents for chronic pain management follow-up (third visit). Pain intensity has decreased and is more tolerable with the current treatment strategy. Sleep has improved. Mood has improved, and anxiety is described as improved compared with the prior visit. She remains worried about pain control.

She underwent a GI study, and a small bowel procedure may be pending. She is worried about GI-related pain and potential post-procedure pain. She notes no signs of withdrawal and no significant short-term exacerbation of pain with associated anxiety episodes. Since the last visit, topiramate was decreased from 200 mg/day to 100 mg/day; she is currently taking topiramate 50 mg BID. She is also taking lamotrigine 200 mg BID prescribed by her psychiatrist.

OBJECTIVE
Physical Exam:
General Appearance: Spontaneously smiling
HEENT: No facial weakness, no pupillary dilation
Neck: No rigidity
Respiratory: No chest wall deformity
Abdominal: Soft, benign, abdominal wall tenderness with deep compression
Musculoskeletal: Paraspinal muscle tenderness in mid back and low back, no joint pain, no swelling, no edema
Psychiatric: Improved anxiety compared with last visit

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN
54-55-year-old female with chronic pain and long-standing opioid exposure with improved pain tolerance, sleep, and mood on an opioid taper strategy using methadone with adjunct clonidine and escitalopram; ongoing paraspinal tenderness and abdominal wall tenderness with concern for pain management needs around a possible upcoming small bowel procedure.

1. CHRONIC PAIN
Improved pain intensity and tolerability on current regimen; paraspinal tenderness on exam, abdominal wall tenderness with deep compression
- Continue clonidine
- Perform manual therapy in clinic including deep muscle massage, acupuncture, and stretching
- Train patient and family member on deep muscle massage techniques for home use
- Encourage nonpharmacologic pain management strategies
- Advise patient to communicate with clinic if GI procedure occurs or if post-procedure pain develops

2. LONG TERM OPIOID THERAPY
Chronic opioid exposure for at least 20 years; short-acting opioids have been discontinued, tapering methadone with reported improvement and no withdrawal symptoms; difficulty tapering from methadone 0.5 mg TID to BID
- Counsel on opioid education and avoidance of unprescribed opioids
- Decrease methadone to 0.5 mg BID for 3 weeks, then decrease to 0.5 mg daily

3. ANXIETY DISORDER
Improved anxiety and mood on current approach; medication optimization discussed
- Increase escitalopram from 5 mg BID to 10 mg BID
- Discontinue paroxetine
- Encourage continuation of lamotrigine 200 mg BID as prescribed by psychiatry

4. HEADACHE
No abnormal headache symptoms noted today; continuing gradual wean of topiramate from prior dose reduction
- Continue topiramate 50 mg BID

Follow-up/Disposition:
- Follow up in 4 weeks
- Close monitoring during medication changes
